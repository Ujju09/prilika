Metric Definitions — Add these to any accounting dashboard
Geometric Mean Income
exp(mean of log rates)
What you actually compound at — always lower than arithmetic mean when there is volatility. The gap between arithmetic and geometric mean is the hidden cost of income variability.
Volatility Penalty
variance / 2
The exact amount volatility subtracts from your compounding rate. From Kelly: geometric mean = arithmetic mean − (variance ÷ 2). Reducing volatility is as powerful as increasing income.
Income Floor (p10)
10th percentile of monthly income
The income level you can expect 90% of months to exceed. Size every fixed obligation against this number. Planning against the average means you're exposed 50% of the time.
3-Month Drift Signal
avg(last 3 log rates)
When this turns negative for 2+ consecutive readings, something structural has changed — not a bad month. This is your early warning system. No natural number shows this.
Income-Expense Divergence
income log rate − expense log rate
Positive = income growing faster than expenses (margin expanding). Negative = expense growth outpacing income (margin compressing silently). A standard P&L won't show the compounding direction.
Recovery Lag
shock log magnitude ÷ avg monthly log rate
How many months of normal growth are needed to mathematically recover from a negative shock. Measures the real cost of your worst month in time, not money.
